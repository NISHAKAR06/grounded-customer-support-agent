Methodology & System Specifications

Formal mathematical formulations and architectural constraints governing grounding and safe escalation.

1. Grounding-First Generation Principle
Strict Architectural Rule

A response $\hat{y}$ generated for an incoming customer message $q$ is defined as Grounded if and only if:

Claims(ŷ) ⊆ Claims(q) ∪ Claims(E)

Where $E = \{e_1, e_2, \dots, e_k\}$ is the set of top-$k$ retrieved historical resolved support interactions from the brand, and $\text{Claims}(\cdot)$ extracts factual commitments (e.g., refund policies, turnaround times, troubleshooting procedures).

Any claim $c \in \text{Claims}(\hat{y})$ that cannot be mapped to $E$ or $q$ constitutes an ungrounded hallucination and is blocked by the ResponseValidator.

2. Escalation Policy Decision Matrix
Deterministic Formulation

The routing decision $D \in \{\text{AUTO\_HANDLE}, \text{HUMAN\_ESCALATION}\}$ is computed via the boolean evaluation:

D = AUTO_HANDLE   if   [P(y = c_intent | q) ≥ τ_intent] ∧ [max_{e ∈ E} cos(e, q) ≥ τ_retrieval] ∧ [¬RiskTrigger(q)] ∧ [ValidatorPassed(ŷ)]
D = HUMAN_ESCALATION   otherwise
  • $\tau_{\text{intent}}$: Minimum calibrated intent classifier confidence (default: 0.85).
  • $\tau_{\text{retrieval}}$: Minimum cosine similarity of top historical case (default: 0.65).
  • $\text{RiskTrigger}(q)$: Boolean function detecting explicit human agent requests, legal threats, or severe dispute language.
  • $\text{ValidatorPassed}(\hat{y})$: Output of deterministic non-empty, claim-bounded validation check.
